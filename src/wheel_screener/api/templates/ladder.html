Call ladder

Covered calls on shares you were assigned, stepped up a set of strikes (the rungs) until the shares can leave at what they cost.

  1. Enter the shares, the price they were assigned at, and the call you sold, if one is open.
  2. The page plans the rungs from the chain: from that call up to your cost, in steps a roll still pays for. Then it writes out the two orders to place at the broker: a take-profit, and a roll up to the next rung that waits for the share price.
  3. When an order fills or the call expires, enter the new call under Edit numbers, and place the next two orders the page gives.

"If called here" is against today's real cost, {{ p.real_cost|money }}. Every premium and roll credit lowers it, so those numbers improve as the ladder climbs.

Rungs left empty are planned when the ladder is saved, and then kept, since orders rest on them. They run from the call you sold (with none open, the strike at delta 0.15 with the shares 15% or more under the real cost, 0.25 within 5–15%, 0.40 closer) up to the first strike at or above your {{ p.cost|money }} cost. The steps are the fewest even ones where each roll still pays the minimum, even if {{ p.symbol }} reaches the trigger the day after a call is sold. To plan them again from today's prices, clear Rungs under Edit numbers and save.

A steady path has none of {{ p.symbol }}'s swings: on it every call decays, so the take-profits all fill, and the plan doesn't add up the premiums, which would be a best case. On real prices some calls end in the money, rolls come sooner or later, and the dates move: the plan is the order of things, and about when.

The rules, as above: a take-profit at 25%, a roll up a rung at 97% of the strike to the shortest expiry four to six weeks on, no call over a report (reports past the next are taken to be a quarter apart), and each new call at the lowest rung above the price and its roll zone, never below the rung reached, if it pays 1% of the price.
